 Mansa Musa's Wealth & Legacy

Mansa Musa (c. 1280–1337), the 14th-century ruler of the Mali Empire, is often regarded as **the wealthiest person in human history**, 
with modern estimates valuing his fortune at over \[$400 billion\] adjusted for inflation. Here's a breakdown of his extraordinary riches and legacy:

---

## 🌍 Sources of Wealth
1. **Gold Monopoly**  
   - Controlled 50% of Old World's gold supply through mines like:
     - Bambuk (Senegal River Valley)
     - Bure (Upper Niger)
     - Akan (modern Ghana)
   - Annual gold output: ~1 ton (equivalent to \[6.5 billion USD\] today)

2. **Salt Trade**  
   - Dominated Saharan salt mines (Taghaza)
   - Salt slabs served as currency in W. Africa

3. **Trans-Saharan Trade**  
   - Taxed caravans carrying:
     - Ivory → Europe  
     *Example*: Single elephant tusk = \[$100,000\] value today
     - Kola nuts → Islamic world

---

## 🕌 Legendary Hajj (1324–1325)
His pilgrimage to Mecca became a **global economic event**:
- Caravan: 60,000 people (incl. 12,000 slaves)
- Gold distributed: 18–24 tons  
  *Result*: Caused 10-year gold price collapse in Cairo
- Built a mosque every Friday during journey

---

## 📜 Economic Impact
| Metric | Effect |  
|--------|--------|
| Cairo gold price | Dropped 25% (took 12 years to recover) |
| Mediterranean trade | Tripled Mali's commercial reach |
| Islamic scholarship | Founded Sankore University (Timbuktu) |

---

## Modern Comparisons
- **Jeff Bezos' peak wealth**: \[$200 billion\] (half of Mansa Musa's)
- **Mali's current GDP**: \[$20.7 billion\] (0.005% of his wealth)

---

## Key Lessons
1. Resource control > Industrial production (pre-modern wealth model)
2. Strategic philanthropy enhanced geopolitical influence
3. Cultural investment (libraries, mosques) created lasting legacy

*Note*: Exact wealth quantification remains debated, but contemporary Arab chroniclers like Al-Umari documented his "unimaginable splendor."

# Age of Gold: Mansa Musa's Legacy  
**Concept**: A real-time strategy game blending *Age of Empires* mechanics with 14th-century West African economics, faith, and diplomacy.  

---

## 🏰 Core Gameplay Mechanics  
### 1. **Unique Resources**  
- **Gold Dust** (primary currency):  
  - Mine from Bure/Bambuk deposits  
  - Convert to **Gold Ingots** (1 ingot = 1000 dust) for advanced units  
- **Salt Blocks**: Mined from Taghaza, used for troop stamina  
- **Manuscripts**: Religious/cultural resource from Timbuktu libraries  

### 2. **Civilization Bonuses**  
| Trait | Effect |  
|-------|--------|  
| **Sankore Scholars** | Technologies unlock 20% faster per built mosque |  
| **Golden Caravans** | Trade routes yield 2x gold when crossing deserts |  
| **Griot Diplomacy** | Convert enemy units with musical "epic" meter |  

---

## ⚔️ Unique Units & Tech Tree  
**Military Units**:  
- **Camel Lancers** (anti-cavalry): 15% speed boost in deserts  
- **Donson Ton** (elite guards): Wield poisoned spears (-3 HP/sec)  
- **Griot Bards**: AoE morale boost (+15% attack)  

**Economic Wonders**:  
- **Great Mosque of Djenné**: Unlocks Timbuktu University (free manuscripts)  
- **Taghaza Salt Mine**: Global salt monopoly (enemies pay 300% salt tax)  

**Tech Upgrades**:  
- **Mansa's Hajj**: All units gain +2 vision (unlocked after building 5 mosques)  
- **Gold Inflation**: Enemy civilizations lose 1 gold/sec per 1000 ingots you hold  

---

## 🌍 Campaign Storylines  
### Chapter 1: **Sand & Sovereignty**  
- Mission 1: Secure Bambuk gold mines from Songhai raiders  
- Mission 3: Build 3 desert trade outposts before Tuareg rebellion  

### Chapter 3: **Pilgrimage Paradox** (1324 Hajj)  
- Objective: Escort 60,000 civilians to Mecca while:  
  - Distributing 18 tons of gold without crashing local economies  
  - Defending against Mamluk bandits  
  - Building a Friday Mosque every 7 game days  

---

## 🕹️ Multiplayer Modes  
1. **Trans-Saharan Showdown**:  
   - 4 players compete to control gold/salt nodes  
   - Random sandstorms disable units (except camel riders)  

2. **Scholars of Sankore**:  
   - Victory by completing 5 manuscript techs before rivals  

---

## 🎨 Art/Sound Design Notes  
- **Architecture**: Sudanese-style mud mosques with protruding timbers  
- **Unit Voices**: Bambara language with Arabic loanwords  
- **Soundtrack**: Kora harp mixed with djembe war rhythms

# Age of Gold: Campaign Missions & Scripts  
**Design Philosophy**: Blend historical events with RTS mechanics, introducing new gameplay elements progressively.  

---

## Chapter 1: **Sand Chiefdoms**  
### Mission 2: *Salt of the Sahara*  
**Objective**: Establish first trans-Saharan salt route  
- **Primary**:  
  1. Escort 3 salt caravans from Taghaza to Walata  
  2. Build 2 desert outposts (200 Gold + 100 Salt each)  
- **Secondary**:  
  - Reach Walata in under 15 minutes (unlocks Camel Lancers)  
**Scripted Event**:  
- *Sandstorm!*: At 8:00 mark, visibility drops 80%; only camel units can move  
**Failure Condition**: Lose all caravans  

---

## Chapter 2: **Mali Ascendancy**  
### Mission 4: *Walls of Timbuktu*  
**Objective**: Conquer the scholarly city  
- **Primary**:  
  1. Breach northern gates using Siege Ladders tech  
  2. Capture Sankore Library (hold for 5 mins)  
- **Secondary**:  
  - Convert 3 enemy scholars using Griot Bards  
**Tech Lock**: Poisoned Spears auto-researched at mission start  
**Victory Cutscene**: Scholars begin copying manuscripts (+1/min)  

---

### Mission 6: *Niger's Fury*  
**Objective**: Destroy Songhai river fleet  
- **Primary**:  
  1. Build 10 war canoes (100 Wood + 50 Gold each)  
  2. Sink 8 enemy dhows  
- **Secondary**:  
  - Protect fishing boats (lose ≤2)  
**Mechanic**:  
- River currents slow units; attack during "calm periods"  
**Failure Condition**: Lose all docks  

---

## Chapter 3: **Golden Hajj**  
### Mission 7: *Pilgrimage Paradox*  
**Objective**: Reach Mecca without crashing economies  
- **Primary**:  
  1. Distribute ≤18 tons gold (meter stays in "green")  
  2. Build 4 Friday Mosques en route  
- **Secondary**:  
  - Avoid Mamluk territory (red zones on map)  
**Dynamic Economy**:  
- Each gold gift temporarily lowers local unit costs  
- Overspending triggers bandit raids  

---

### Mission 9: *Crescent and Cross*  
**Final Objective**: Establish global economic hegemony  
- **Primary**:  
  1. Control 5/7 wonders (e.g., Alhambra, Notre Dame)  
  2. Maintain 2000+ Gold Ingots for 10 mins  
- **Secondary**:  
  - Convert Pope to Islam (Griot Diplomacy minigame)  
**Endgame Mechanic**:  
- Enemy alliances form against you  
- Golden Mansa hero unit becomes vulnerable  

---

## Bonus Missions  
### *Scholars' Revolt* (Skirmish)  
**Twist**: Manuscripts spawn rebel armies  
- Destroy rogue libraries before they spread  
**Reward**: Unlock "Ancient Algorithms" tech  

---

## Scripting Notes  
1. **AI Behavior**:  
   - Enemies prioritize attacking:  
     - Gold caravans (60% probability)  
     - Unprotected mosques (30%)  
     - Hero units (10%)  

2. **Dynamic Dialog**:  
   - Mansa Musa voice lines change based on gold reserves:  
     - Wealthy: "The desert itself kneels before Mali!"  
     - Poor: "Even salt loses its savor without gold."  

3. **Failure Branches**:  
   - Losing Mission 5 triggers *Salt Famine* alternate timeline  
   - Failing Hajj creates *Mamluk Mali* faction
